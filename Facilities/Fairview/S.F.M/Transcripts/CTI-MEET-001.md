# CITRUS TECHNOLOGIES INC. / PARAFIELD CORP.
## MEETING TRANSCRIPT: S.F.M x20 DEMONSTRATION — FAIRVIEW RESEARCH COMPLEX
**Document Ref:** CTI-MEET-001
**Date:** March 21, 2013
**Location:** Fairview Research Complex, Reactor Wing — Observation Level
**Participants:**
- Marcus Hale, Senior Research Director, Citrus Technologies Inc.
- Dr. Yuna Calloway, Lead Engineer, Citrus Technologies Inc.
- Batista, Chief Executive Officer, Parafield Corp.
- Randi Mathias, Site Director, Meridian Depthworks Centre
- Simo Edward, Site Director, Lambda Research Complex

---

[TRANSCRIPT BEGINS — 10:15 AM]

**HALE:** Glad you all made it. Dr. Calloway is going to walk you through the engineering side once we're on the floor. Before we head down I want to give you the short version of how we got here.

**BATISTA:** Go ahead.

**HALE:** After the Meridian situation last year we had a few of our guys revisiting the isotope data we had on file from the NatElec agreement. Nothing structured, just looking. Calloway's team started running proximity tests on solid-state derivatives — not pulse ignition, just seeing how the material behaved under sustained close contact. Six weeks in they're getting a stable thermal output with no laser involvement whatsoever.

**MATHIAS:** No ignition at all?

**CALLOWAY:** None. The lattice structure in the compound does the work once the rods are close enough to each other. We weren't expecting it either — the original tests were meant to run for two days. We just never had to shut it off.

**EDWARD:** How close are we talking?

**CALLOWAY:** Critical proximity sits between twenty and eighty centimeters depending on the output target. Below twenty and you're pushing into runaway territory. Above eighty and the exchange rate drops off, reaction slows to idle.

**BATISTA:** And the water jacket keeps it from climbing on its own?

**CALLOWAY:** That's the key difference from the S.F.R. The water moderates the neutron flux passively. If the temperature spikes, moderation increases automatically and the reaction slows down. It self-corrects. You'd have to actively work against the system to cause a serious incident.

**MATHIAS:** That's a significant change from what we've been dealing with at Meridian.

**HALE:** That's why we called you in. We're not trying to replace the S.F.R — the output ceiling here is nowhere near what the Mark I produces. But for a facility like Fairview running on research loads, this thing is more than enough and it doesn't need a full reactor operations crew babysitting it around the clock.

[GROUP MOVES TO OBSERVATION LEVEL — 10:31 AM]

**CALLOWAY:** What you're looking at through the glass is the x20 prototype. Twenty rods, four clusters of five. Right now we're running Clusters A and B at sixty centimeter spacing. That's about fifty percent output.

**EDWARD:** It's quiet.

**CALLOWAY:** That's the other thing. No Hex-Triad charging cycle, no discharge hum. Just the coolant pumps.

**BATISTA:** What's the worst case look like if something goes wrong?

**CALLOWAY:** Three tier response. SCRAM first, handled by control room or the facility AI if nobody moves within fifteen seconds. If that doesn't bring the NFI down, hydraulic arms separate all twenty rods mechanically — spring-loaded, no power required. Last resort is boron injection straight into the water jacket. Kills the reaction chemically. Reactor needs a full decontamination afterward but nothing's going critical.

**BATISTA:** No ejection procedure. No synchronization window.

**CALLOWAY:** No. Worst day here is a six week decontamination. Not a superstructure event.

**MATHIAS:** *(quietly)* God.

**HALE:** We wanted you to see it running before we talked about anything formal. Where you take it from here is your call. We're not looking to license this back to you — the isotope IP is yours. We just figured if anyone needed to know this was possible, it was Parafield.

**BATISTA:** I appreciate that Marcus. I genuinely do. *(pause)* Simo, Randi — thoughts?

**EDWARD:** From a Lambda standpoint this is worth serious consideration. If we can run a facility like this on sustained output without the operational overhead, that changes our staffing model significantly.

**MATHIAS:** Meridian isn't ready for anything new right now. We're still in cleanup. But down the line — yeah, I'd want this looked at.

**BATISTA:** Alright. Let's get your full technical documentation and take it back. We'll get the right people reading it and go from there.

**HALE:** Already packaged. Calloway will send it through the NatElec channel before end of day.

**BATISTA:** Good. Thank you both. Seriously — this is good work.

**HALE:** That's what we're here for.

**BATISTA:** Before we leave — I want to put something on the table. I'm not ready to greenlight anything based on a controlled prototype run. If this is as solid as you're saying, prove it under real load.

**HALE:** What are you thinking?

**BATISTA:** I'm going to contact Fairview's city grid administration. As Prime Minister of United Columbus I can authorize a temporary interconnection — hook the x20 directly into Fairview's municipal power network for three weeks. Real draw, real demand, no safety net from a backup source.

**CALLOWAY:** That's... a significant step.

**BATISTA:** It is. And if the reactor runs clean for three weeks with no faults outside of anything a human operator caused, Parafield will formally declare the project a success and authorize Citrus to move forward however you see fit. If there's a root cause issue — something in the design, the rods, the PLR behavior under sustained grid load — we'll flag it and you go back and find it.

**HALE:** And if it's operator error?

**BATISTA:** Operator error doesn't count against the machine. That's on personnel, not engineering. The reactor has to fail on its own for us to have a problem.

**HALE:** *(to Calloway)* Yuna?

**CALLOWAY:** I'm not worried about the reactor. Three weeks on a live grid is exactly the kind of data we'd want anyway.

**HALE:** Then we accept. When do you want to start the clock?

**BATISTA:** Give me a few days to sort the grid authorization. Call it March 25th.

**HALE:** March 25th it is.

**BATISTA:** Good. We'll be watching the data remotely. Randi, Simo — anything before we head out?

**EDWARD:** Nothing from me.

**MATHIAS:** Just — good luck. Hope it holds.

**CALLOWAY:** It will.

[TRANSCRIPT ENDS — 11:24 AM]
